## MethodsX requirements and precedents

MethodsX is a template-locked journal: the Method Article Template v7 (Sept 2024) is mandatory, section headings cannot be changed, and every section is required unless marked OPTIONAL. The hard constraints that bite this project: **Results and Discussion are not article sections** — the 0.6953 → 0.9340 headline must be written as "Method validation", not as a finding; Background is capped at 500 words; abstract 200 words with 1-3 bullets; a graphical abstract is mandatory as a separate file (531 × 1328 px min); references are guided to ~25; and **Limitations is a mandatory section** whose stated purpose is "experimental conditions in which the method may not work."

The single most consequential policy is in the reviewer guidelines: *"It is journal policy to just allow text revisions. If additional experiments are required for a manuscript to become suitable for publication, we will reject it."* There is no major-revision path. This means the C. pogonias 5th class and any audio-pretrained backbone comparison must either be finished before submission or be explicitly and defensibly scoped out in Limitations — because a reviewer who wants either one added triggers rejection, not revision. Reviewers are asked only three substantive questions, and none of them is about novelty or performance: are the steps logical and detailed enough; can others reproduce it; will it be useful to others in the field. That is the entire bar.

This strongly favors the project. The closed-set congener failure and the domain shift are *not* weaknesses under MethodsX criteria — they are exactly what the mandatory Limitations section asks for, and the 2025-26 accepted papers I examined write limitations in precisely that register (one states plainly that "external validation remains essential for broader generalization"). What would sink the paper is under-specification of the procedure, not an imperfect detector.

Calibration from six comparable papers: accepted MethodsX ML-for-ecology articles run **~2,000-4,000 words of body text, 5-7 figures, 2-3 tables, 14-25 references**. The validation bar is startlingly low — ATfiltR's entire "Test of the method" is two sentences describing four animal detections across three receivers. A 16-fold LOSO design scored against human ear review is far beyond anything in this comparison set and should be positioned as the paper's distinguishing strength.

Two positioning facts worth acting on. First, MethodsX has published essentially **no bioacoustics deep-learning papers** — systematic Europe PMC and Crossref searches of the journal returned zero. The closest domain match (AudioMoth PAM in a Cameroonian tropical forest, 2022) is *entirely manual listening with no classifier*, so this submission is a clear advance over the journal's own state of the art in its exact domain. Second, the reusable procedure — the contribution MethodsX actually buys — is best framed not as "a VGG19 primate detector" but as the **LOSO-validated post-processing chain** (0.4096 → 0.7009 → 0.869 via time gate plus temporal isolation filter). ATfiltR is direct precedent that a filtering-and-cleaning pipeline is publishable as a method in its own right, and Kershenbaum et al. (2025) independently names behavioural-prior filtering as a legitimate technique *and* names its risk, giving both a citation and a ready-made Limitations paragraph.

**MethodsX Method Article Template, Version 7 (September 2024) — the mandatory submission format**

- Official .docx template, downloaded and read verbatim. States: "We will only consider methods articles submitted using this template" and "Please do not change any section headings." All sections mandatory unless marked OPTIONAL. Hard limits: Title max 20 words; Abstract max 200 words including 1-3 bullet points that "outline your method – avoid the technical steps"; Background max 500 words; Method details has NO word limit; Method validation states "Results and Discussion are not part of a methods article. However, if possible, please provide data that validate the method"; Limitations is mandatory ('None' or 'Not applicable' if genuinely none) and is explicitly for "experimental conditions in which the method may not work." Specifications table fields: Subject area; More specific subject area; Name of your method; Name and reference of original method; Resource availability. Graphical abstract mandatory, separate file, min 531 × 1328 px (h × w), readable at 5 × 13 cm at 96 dpi, TIFF/EPS/PDF/MS Office. Also required: Related research article (one only, or "None"), Ethics statements, CRediT, Declaration of interests. References section note: "If your methods article is about a customization you made, include at least one reference to the original publication of that method." A separate protocol template exists for clinical/life-science protocols. Editorial contact mex-me@elsevier.com.
- Use here: This is the skeleton to write into today. Three direct consequences. (1) The headline LOSO numbers cannot be a Results section — they go under 'Method validation'. Reframe 0.6953 → 0.9340 as evidence the procedure works, not as a scientific finding. (2) 'Name and reference of original method' should cite Simonyan & Zisserman (VGG) — the paper is formally a *customization* of ImageNet transfer learning to PAM spectrograms, which is precisely MethodsX's stated sweet spot. (3) Background is only 500 words: the Ivindo/Cercopithecus nictitans motivation must be compressed hard, with all pipeline detail pushed into the unlimited Method details section. **MethodsX Reviewer Guidelines — the actual accept/reject criteria, and the no-new-experiments policy**

- Primary source, extracted verbatim from the Elsevier reviewer form. Opens: "MethodsX publishes small but important customizations of methods, with the intent to provide technical information useful to researchers working in the field. It should be clear what changes over the status quo are being presented, and how their validity has been tested. We ask authors and reviewers to focus on the technical aspects of the work." Critically: "It is journal policy to just allow text revisions. If additional experiments are required for a manuscript to become suitable for publication, we will reject it." Reviewers answer only three substantive yes/no questions: (1) Are the technical steps logical and described with sufficient detail? (2) Can others reproduce this method based on the protocol(s) provided? (3) Will this information be useful to others working in the field/with this method? They then rate Abstract, Graphical Abstract, Methods, References as very good/acceptable/needs improvement, and recommend Accept / Accept with text revision / Reject. Novelty, effect size, and statistical significance are never asked about.
- Use here: The highest-stakes finding for submission strategy. There is no major-revision path — a reviewer who says 'you should add a C. pogonias class' or 'you should benchmark against an audio-pretrained backbone' causes rejection, not revision. So decide before submitting: either finish the pogonias 5th class and include it, or write a Limitations paragraph that scopes it out so convincingly no reviewer asks. The same applies to the untested audio backbones. Conversely, this is liberating: the congener failure and the zero-guereza domain-shift result cannot fail criteria 1-3, because those criteria only ask whether the procedure is detailed, reproducible, and useful. Write to those three questions literally — a numbered, parameter-complete protocol (window 2 s, stride 1 s, 224x224x3, exact librosa calls, exact time gate 05:00-19:00, exact neighbour-count rule and how it was LOSO-fitted).
- https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/751614reviewer_guidelines.pdf

**Comparable paper 1 — Methods to measure biological sounds and assess their drivers in a tropical forest (MethodsX, 2022)**

- The closest published domain match in the entire journal: AudioMoth-based PAM in Southeast Cameroon (Dja Faunal Reserve), across a disturbance gradient. Also borrow its three-bullet abstract pattern and its specifications-table wording (Environmental Science / Ecoacoustics) almost verbatim.

**Comparable paper 2 — ATfiltR: managing and filtering detections from passive acoustic telemetry data (MethodsX, 2023)**

- An R package that removes spurious detections from acoustic telemetry via a chain of filters, each documented as its own subsection: compileData(), wWindow(), findSolo(), speedCheck(), toActel(). findSolo is a temporal-isolation filter — its abstract bullet reads "As spurious detections are unlikely to appear sequentially in the data, ATfiltR finds all detections that occurred only once (per receiver or in the whole array) within a user-designated time period and stores them elsewhere." Body ~3,780 words, 7 figures, 2 tables, 14 references. The specifications table cites prior work as the 'original method' (Simpfendorfer et al. 2015 on false detections; Gupte et al. 2022 on pre-processing tracking data). Its entire method validation, titled "Test of the method", is two sentences: a realistic test dataset of four animal detections across three receivers, with code in an appendix. Published before template v7; no Limitations section.
- Use here: Direct precedent that a false-positive filtering chain is publishable in MethodsX as a method in its own right — which argues for making the post-processing chain (0.4096 → 0.7009 → 0.869) the paper's headline contribution rather than a footnote to the classifier. Its findSolo filter is conceptually the same object as the temporal isolation filter, so cite it as prior art and state what is customized (LOSO-fitted neighbour threshold rather than user-designated). Adopt its one-subsection-per-filter-stage layout for Method details. Its two-sentence validation also calibrates expectations: a 16-fold LOSO against human review is vastly above the journal's demonstrated bar, and should be foregrounded, not hedged.
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mex.2023.102222

**Comparable paper 3 — A protocol for developing a classification system of mosquitoes using transfer learning (MethodsX, 2023)**

- Closest methodological analogue: frozen-backbone transfer learning for species classification by a domain scientist. Compares MobileNetV2 and VGG16 across a hyperparameter grid (batch size 32/64, learning rate 0.001/0.0005) on two Aedes species. Structure: Description of protocol / Programming tools / Dataset / Data augmentation and data splitting / Transfer learning model construction / Protocol validation. Body only ~2,170 words, 6 figures, 2 tables, 14 references. Validation reports per-configuration accuracy/precision/sensitivity, confusion matrices, and training/validation curves — MobileNetV2 reached at least 0.96 on all metrics at lr 0.001; VGG16 reached at least 0.98 sensitivity across most settings. Explicitly frames itself as being for domain experts: "This protocol target on the domain expert such as entomologist and public health practices to develop their own model." Notes that most prior mosquito work used VGG16/VGG19 and that MobileNetV2 outperformed VGG16 at batch size 64. No Limitations section (pre-v7).
- Use here: Proof that a VGG-class frozen-backbone pipeline with only two classes and a few thousand images clears the MethodsX bar, at 2,170 words. Two lessons to copy: state the target reader explicitly ("primatologists and PAM practitioners without ML training"), and report the hyperparameter grid rather than only the best configuration — that is what makes the procedure reusable rather than a result. It also shows the field's reflex to reach for VGG, which is useful cover for the VGG19 choice while the Limitations section flags audio-pretrained backbones as untested.
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mex.2022.101947

**Kershenbaum et al. (2025), Automatic detection for bioacoustic research: a practical guide — Biological Reviews**

- The current standard reference for PAM detection methodology, open access. Directly addresses four of the five known problems, including where approaches fail. On data splitting, it endorses exactly this project's design: "Creating an effective test data set may include the selection of a separate microphone entry, specific time frames, separate recording locations... We always want to ensure that an automatic detection model is generalisable rather than specifically trained for a single recording setup, location, or individual." On reporting: "converting the precision into the number of false positives per hour allows an unambiguous interpretation by the user and the planning of how to deal with false alarms." On behavioural-prior filtering — and this is the explicit caveat — "prior knowledge of vocal behaviour, such as sequence regularities, might allow filtering out of false positives. Such priors can be used to reduce confidence thresholds and increase the recall, but with the risk of imposing too strong priors and missing out on uncommon sequences." On congeners: "it can be difficult to distinguish vocalisations of similar species if they share characteristics." On augmentation, a warning that matters given tiny classes plus congeners: "care must be taken to avoid transformations that could invalidate the annotations. For instance, in a bird call detector, reversing sounds could be a tempting simple transformation, yet this could result in artificially making a bird call more similar to that of another species." On denoising/preprocessing: "this pre-processing step is not always recommended and must be used with caution as it may result in a loss of information." It also points to HEAR (Turian et al. 2022) and BEANS (Hagiwara et al. 2022) as the benchmarks for choosing pretrained audio representations, and warns that fast-moving pretrained models are often used "despite having been superseded by other options."
- Use here: The single most useful citation for this paper — it supplies literature backing for four separate sections. It justifies LOSO as best practice rather than an idiosyncratic choice (cite it in Method details when defining folds). It says to report false positives per hour alongside precision — add that to the validation table; for 630+ hours of deployment audio it is a one-line calculation and it is what a PAM reader actually needs. Most importantly it both legitimises the time gate and isolation filter *and* names their failure mode, giving a ready-made, citable Limitations paragraph: strong temporal priors risk discarding genuine off-schedule or isolated calls, which is a real cost for a species that may vocalise outside 05:00-19:00. It also supplies the counterargument to the null guereza result (detection range and habitat attenuation confound absence) and a caution about whether heavy augmentation of the 172 guereza clips pushed them toward congener acoustics.
- https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.13155

**Ghani et al. (2023), Global birdsong embeddings enable superior transfer learning for bioacoustic classification — Scientific Reports**

- Tests whether embeddings from large bird-audio classifiers transfer to taxa they were never trained on, evaluated across bird calls and dialects, bat calls, marine mammal calls and amphibian calls, using a linear probe on frozen embeddings and scoring macro-averaged ROC-AUC and Top-1 accuracy. Finding: "The embeddings extracted from the models trained on bird vocalization data consistently allowed higher quality classification than the embeddings trained on general audio datasets." Specifically, BirdNET and Perch "consistently outperformed general event-detection models trained on broader auditory data, such as YouTube-sourced data utilized by AudioMAE, PSLA, YAMNet, and VGGish." Conclusion: high-quality embeddings from bird classifiers "can be harnessed for few-shot transfer learning, enabling the learning of new classes from a limited quantity of training data." Important scope caveat to state accurately: the comparison set is audio-pretrained models against each other — the paper does not benchmark against ImageNet-pretrained image CNNs such as VGG19, so it does not directly prove an ImageNet backbone is worse.
- Use here: The evidence base for both the backbone question and the tiny-class question, and it should shape what is promised in Limitations. It supports a specific, concrete 'future work' sentence — replace the VGG19/ImageNet base with frozen Perch or BirdNET embeddings plus a linear probe — that is credible because it is the documented state of practice, without requiring any new experiment before submission (which the no-new-experiments policy makes essential). It is also the strongest argument for how to handle the 172-clip guereza class and the handful of hand-cut C. pogonias clips: few-shot linear probing on bird-pretrained embeddings is designed for exactly that regime, whereas training a VGG19 head on 172 augmented archival clips is not. Cite it honestly as motivating, not as proof that VGG19 underperforms — the direct comparison has not been run here.
- https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-49989-z

**Stowell (2022), Computational bioacoustics with deep learning: a review and roadmap — PeerJ**

- The foundational review of deep learning in bioacoustics and still the standard general citation. Reviews the state of the art, notes that although methods are inherited from speech and image processing, "the tasks, demands and data characteristics are often different from those addressed in speech or music analysis," and identifies remaining unsolved problems and knowledge gaps, offering a roadmap of what the community should address.

## Open-set / congener confusion

Bottom line for the MethodsX paper: what you observed (C. pogonias scoring 1.0000 on the Cernic channel; a second Colobus species scoring 1.0000 on the Colobus channel) is the canonical "near-OOD / fine-grained open-set" failure, and the literature is unusually consistent that **no post-hoc score fixes it**. Four findings dominate.

(1) THE DIAGNOSIS IS NAMED AND QUANTIFIED. Open-Insect (NeurIPS 2025, 2.9M images / 28,388 moth species, 38 OOD algorithms) is the closest published analogue to your problem: novel species that are taxonomically and geographically *local* (i.e. congeners in the same community) are detected at 80-87% AUROC, versus 87-95% for non-local species and 89-91% for non-moth arthropods. Several fashionable methods collapse outright on local species (GradNorm 22-50% AUROC — worse than chance; OpenGAN 31-52%). Their conclusion, echoed by Vaze et al. (ICLR 2022) and OpenOOD v1.5, is that plain max-softmax/max-logit remains a competitive baseline and the 26 fancier post-hoc scores buy little on fine-grained semantic novelty. MixOE (WACV 2023) states the mechanism directly: even a diverse auxiliary outlier set "does not provide sufficient coverage over the broad region where fine-grained OOD samples locate." So do not build the paper's contribution on OpenMax/ODIN/Mahalanobis/energy — the literature predicts small, unstable gains exactly in your regime, and OpenOOD v1.5 explicitly reports that no single post-hoc method wins across benchmarks.

(2) THE FIX WITH EVIDENCE IS TO CONVERT UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS INTO KNOWN UNKNOWNS. This is the "known unknowns vs unknown unknowns" distinction (Boult et al., AAAI 2019; Dhamija et al., NeurIPS 2018). Every intervention that works in the literature does so by putting *labelled* negative material of the right kind into training. Open-Insect: curated region-specific auxiliary data +3.28 pp AUROC on local (congeneric) novelty, while swapping in generic TinyImageNet outliers *loses* 4.82 pp — domain-relevant negatives matter, generic ones actively hurt. Bisgin/Günther et al. (2024, large-scale OSR evaluation) find training-time Entropic Open-Set (EOS) improves almost every post-processor, but also warn that all methods "perform poorly when tested on samples of previously unseen unknown classes" — i.e. a negative class generalises to the negatives you gave it, not beyond. Your `Colobus_confuser` class is precisely this design and this is precisely its predicted limitation: it will suppress the confuser it was cut from, not a new congener. Adding C. pogonias as a 5th class is therefore the single best-supported move you can make, and the paper should frame it as such rather than as a patch.

(3) CALIBRATION IS NOT A FIX, AND ONE POPULAR CALIBRATION TRICK MAKES IT WORSE. Temperature scaling (Guo et al. 2017) is fitted on in-distribution validation data and says nothing about a class that has no label; reported gains in OOD AUROC over MSP are at most ~1 percentage point, and there are architectures that are provably overconfident regardless of ID calibration. Label smoothing is a documented negative result — it "always deteriorates OOD detection regardless of the magnitude of α," with larger α worse, because it compresses max-logit/max-probability scale and increases known/unknown overlap. Focal loss (Mukhoti et al., NeurIPS 2020) is the one calibration-adjacent *training* change with reported OOD gains as well as lower ECE. For bioacoustics specifically, Schwinger et al. (2025) calibrate multi-label bird classifiers on BirdSet PAM data and find Platt scaling on a small labelled calibration set works well — which is directly transplantable to your LOSO folds. Treat calibration in the paper as a reporting/threshold-setting tool, not as an answer to congeners.

(4) THE BIOACOUSTIC-SPECIFIC LITERATURE IS THIN, AND WHERE IT EXISTS IT RECOMMENDS DISTANCE IN EMBEDDING SPACE, NOT OPENMAX. Morgan & Braasch (JASA 2022) is the reference open-set-in-PAM paper: they compared prediction-score thresholding, distance-based thresholding and OpenMax on environmental field recordings and found the distance threshold best (~62.4% mean accuracy on single-corpus), with performance "highly dependent upon the degree of similarity between the training, testing, and unknown domain" — which is simultaneously your congener problem and your archival-vs-AudioMoth domain-shift problem. Ko et al. (EMBC 2023) is the only dedicated open-set *bioacoustics* method paper I found (Class Anchor Clustering + an explicit closed-set-unknown class, 101 species). Sridhar & Cartwright (DCASE 2023) report only 54-61% unknown-detection accuracy for multi-label open-set audio and conclude existing approaches "cannot adequately solve" it. An ODIN+MC-dropout system on real underwater acoustics reaches AUROC 0.7502. Realistic expectations for a rejection module on field audio are therefore AUROC ~0.75-0.87, not 0.95+.

WHAT I WOULD PUT IN THE METHODS PAPER. A congener-probe protocol is a genuinely reusable MethodsX contribution and is currently under-standardised in PAM: (a) hold out an entire congeneric species as a labelled "known-unknown probe" and report AUROC/FPR@95 of the target channel on it, alongside your LOSO precision — Open-Insect's local/non-local/non-taxon stratification is the template; (b) report the Open Set Classification Rate (OSCR) curve (Dhamija et al. 2018) rather than closed-set macro precision alone; (c) add a distance-to-class-centroid or k-NN score on penultimate features as a cheap second channel, per Morgan & Braasch — and report honestly if it does not help; (d) name your domain-shift confound using the generalized-OOD taxonomy (Yang et al.): archival-library-vs-AudioMoth is *covariate* shift, C. pogonias is *semantic* shift, and your zero-guereza result cannot separate them by construction — say so explicitly, because that is a methodological contribution in itself.

TWO ADJACENT LEVERS WORTH A PARAGRAPH EACH. Backbone: Vaze et al. show open-set rejection ability is strongly correlated with closed-set accuracy across losses and architectures, so replacing frozen VGG19/ImageNet with an audio- or bioacoustics-pretrained encoder is simultaneously the closed-set fix and the open-set fix; Perch 2.0 reports SOTA on BirdSet and BEANS with frozen embeddings + linear probe and transfers to taxa it was barely trained on (it beats specialist marine models with almost no marine training data), so a linear head on Perch embeddings is a low-cost ablation with a strong prior. And it unlocks a validated field workflow: Allen-Ankins et al. (2025) use Euclidean distance in BirdNET embedding space as a nearest-neighbour search for classes with almost no training data (Blue-winged Parrot found at 9/10 sites from a *single* example call) — that is a credible route for guereza's 172 clips and for pogonias's handful of hand-cut clips, and it sidesteps the tiny-class training problem entirely. Post-processing: your time-gate + temporal-isolation chain is well-supported — species-specific thresholds beat a universal 0.7 threshold (precision >0.9 while retaining 70±37% of predictions vs 17±14%), contextual metadata in a multi-branch CNN cut gibbon false positives by 63%, and aggregated time-series features improve true/false positive separation. Also relevant to your "matched 95% recall" operating point: recent open-set plankton work shows FPR@95%-unknown-recall thresholds over-reject *known* taxa and distort community-level estimates, arguing for calibrating thresholds against the ecological quantity rather than the sample-level metric.

CAVEAT ON EVIDENTIAL DEEP LEARNING: do not stake the paper on it. Sensoy et al.'s Dirichlet/EDL framework is attractive on paper, but it has been hit hard since 2023-2024 — its epistemic uncertainties are non-vanishing even with infinite data, second-order losses are not proper scoring rules, and a NeurIPS 2024 paper asks whether its UQ capabilities are "a mirage." **On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks (Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger, ICML 2017) — and why temperature scaling does not solve open-set**

**Evidential deep learning — Sensoy et al. (NeurIPS 2018) and the 2023-2024 critiques (Jürgens et al. ICML 2024; 'Are UQ Capabilities of Evidential Deep Learning a Mirage?' NeurIPS 2024; Bengs et al.)**

- EDL replaces the softmax with a Dirichlet over class probabilities, treating logits as 'evidence' and giving an explicit vacuity/epistemic-uncertainty term intended to fire on unknown inputs. It has been seriously undermined since 2023. Bengs et al. show learned epistemic uncertainties are unreliable and non-vanishing even with infinite data, violating the basic requirement that epistemic uncertainty shrink with more evidence, and that existing second-order losses do not constitute proper scoring rules. Jürgens et al. (ICML 2024) show epistemic uncertainty is not faithfully represented and that the measures are relative rather than absolute. A NeurIPS 2024 paper asks whether the UQ capabilities are 'a mirage'. Audio applications exist (FADEL for fake-audio detection, SEED for sound event early detection) but I found nothing on field bioacoustics.
- Use here: Include for completeness in the related-work survey, but do not make it the paper's method. It is superficially the perfect answer to your problem — an explicit 'I have no evidence for any of my classes' output — and a reviewer may suggest it, so you want the critique citations ready. The honest position: theoretically contested, no field bioacoustic validation, and the same near-OOD limitation applies since a congener generates plenty of Cernic-like evidence.

**One-vs-rest network architectures for open-set recognition (Jang & Kim 2020; Collective Decision of One-vs-Rest Networks, 2021)**

- Replaces the shared softmax with per-class sigmoid heads on top of a common feature extractor. The argument: a sigmoid head is trained explicitly on non-match examples for its class and so learns class-specific discriminative features and more informative hidden representations for unknowns, whereas softmax only ever learns what distinguishes the known classes FROM EACH OTHER and must always allocate probability summing to one. Reported to improve non-match rejection over softmax. Documented limitation: one-vs-rest still suffers negative-set bias, since the 'rest' set at training time is finite while the open universe is not.
- Use here: Architecturally the most important single point for your paper. Softmax over {Cernic, Colobus_guereza, Colobus_confuser, Background} makes 'this is a monkey I have never heard' structurally unrepresentable — the probabilities must sum to 1 across four options, so a pogonias call is forced into the nearest one, and 1.0000 is the expected output, not an anomaly. Four independent sigmoid heads permit the all-low state. This is a small change to your head (frozen VGG19 base untouched), it composes with multi-label windows containing overlapping species, and it is a concrete, reusable procedural recommendation — exactly what a methods journal wants. The negative-set-bias caveat is the honest limitation to state alongside it.

## Audio foundation models vs VGG19

Short version: the literature is close to unanimous that a frozen ImageNet CNN on spectrograms is the weakest reasonable choice in 2026, and that a frozen **bioacoustics** embedding model + a shallow probe is both stronger and better suited to tiny label sets. Ghani et al. (2023, Sci Rep) is the canonical demonstration: with only 32 examples/class, Perch embeddings + logistic regression beat AudioMAE, YAMNet and VGGish on bats, marine mammals and frogs — and VGGish collapsed to 0.04 top-1 on 32-class Watkins marine mammals, i.e. general-audio/image-style features actively fail on non-bird taxa. Perch 2.0 (Aug 2025) is the current default: 1.54M recordings, 14,597 species, EfficientNet-B3, 1536-d embedding, 5 s @ 32 kHz, and it is state of the art on both BirdSet and BEANS. Most important number for you: on the **Hainan Gibbons** detection task in BEANS (a primate, in real PAM), Perch 2.0 with a prototypical probe scores 0.516 mAP vs 0.146 for Perch 1.0, 0.284 for AVES-Bio and 0.005 for NatureLM-audio. There is now also a directly analogous published pipeline — Perch embeddings + a band-pass filter + a One-Class SVM for the pied tamarin — that is *specifically* an open-set answer to your congener problem.

Mapping to your five problems:
(1) **Congener failure** is a recognised open-set-recognition problem, not a bug. Two remedies in the literature: (a) one-class/anomaly scoring on frozen embeddings (pied tamarin paper, MIT-licensed pip package), which never has to name the congener; (b) add acoustically-similar non-target species as explicit negatives — your `Colobus_confuser` class is already this move, and the literature says it raises precision. A third option unique to embedding models: BioLingual/CLAP lets you *query* for "Cercopithecus pogonias" by text with zero training clips.
(2) **Domain shift** has a direct empirical answer in SurfPerch: pretraining on birds alone gave 0.881 AUC-ROC on reef tasks; the target-domain data alone gave 0.724; **mixing** bird + target-domain + unrelated audio gave 0.933. The message for your archival-only Colobus class is that you should mix, not replace, and that a frozen bird-pretrained embedder is a far more channel-robust starting point than a fine-tuned ImageNet base.
(3) **Tiny positive classes** (172 guereza, a handful of pogonias) is the exact regime these embeddings were built for — ROC-AUC well above chance at k=4 examples/class, and Clink's gibbon work reports transfer learning working from ~25 samples.
(4) **Preprocessing**: your PCEN result (+0.0072) matches what Google themselves concluded — Perch 1.0 used PCEN, **Perch 2.0 dropped it for plain log-mel**. Your negative result is publishable and consistent. The bigger point is that switching backbone makes the frontend question moot: you feed raw audio at the model's native rate and the frontend is fixed.
(5) **Backbone**: replace, but as a *frozen embedder + shallow probe*, not a fine-tune. This preserves your existing LOSO structure exactly — you re-run 16 folds with a logistic-regression head over embeddings instead of a dense head over VGG19 features.

Practical route for a MethodsX paper: `bacpipe` (pip install, 24+ models behind one API, built-in linear probing and clustering) is the single most useful tool I found — it turns "which backbone" into a one-line config change and gives you a reusable, citable procedure, which is what MethodsX actually rewards. Caveats worth reporting honestly: BirdNET is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (fine for research, restricts downstream reuse, and any classifier you train inherits it); NatureLM-audio is excellent at zero-shot naming but is a poor *detector*; and transformer models (BEATs, BirdMAE) need attentive probing rather than linear probing to show their full performance, which is more machinery than a linear probe on Perch.

**Mind the Domain Gap: a Systematic Analysis on Bioacoustic Sound Event Detection**

- Studies domain shift between source and target distributions in few-shot bioacoustic SED, attributing it to diverse target sounds, sparse vocalisation distribution, and dynamic acoustic environments from varying microphones, sound sources and recording contexts. Uses a small CNN with prototypical networks and tests two remedies. Results: DCASE 2022 Task 5 with both transductive learning and negative hard sampling — precision 66.43%, recall 61.28%, F1 63.67%; ablation shows transductive learning alone worth +11.58 F1 percentage points. DCASE 2024 with negative hard sampling only — 56.18 / 48.64 / 52.14. Key negative finding: the baseline performs worst on pilot whales, a marine environment unseen during training, confirming that acoustic environments absent from training cause substantial degradation, and that negative hard sampling gives only modest gains without fully bridging the gap.
- Use here: Two transferable techniques for problems 1 and 2. **Negative hard sampling** — mining the hardest negatives rather than sampling background uniformly — is what your `Colobus_confuser` class is doing informally, and this gives you a name, a citation and a systematic procedure for it; the obvious extension is to mine hard negatives automatically from high-scoring false positives in your ear review rather than curating them by hand. **Transductive inference** — using the unlabelled test-fold data at inference time to adapt the decision boundary — maps neatly onto your LOSO setup, where each held-out station is a natural, self-contained target domain with abundant unlabelled audio; +11.58 F1 points is a large effect for a change that requires no new labels. The pilot-whale result is also your honest caveat for problem 2: even with these remedies, an environment genuinely unseen in training degrades badly.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18638

**Applying machine learning to primate bioacoustics: Review and perspectives (Am J Primatol, 2024)**

- Review of computational bioacoustics and signal/speech processing for primate vocal communication, covering supervised algorithms through self-supervised models, in the context of the growth of PAM. Discusses data collection and annotation challenges — small annotated corpora, class imbalance, inter-annotator variability — and surveys solutions, including the adaptation of self-supervised speech models (HuBERT, wav2vec2) to primate vocalisations and adaptation strategies spanning linear probing, fine-tuning and knowledge distillation. doi:10.1002/ajp.23666.
- Use here: Your framing citation for the primatology side of the paper, and useful for positioning: it establishes that the primate bioacoustics field has been slower to adopt foundation models than the bird field, which is precisely the gap your MethodsX contribution fills. Its taxonomy of adaptation strategies (linear probing / fine-tuning / distillation) is also a ready-made structure for your methods section — you can state plainly that you use linear (and prototypical) probing on frozen embeddings, and cite this for why that is the appropriate choice given annotation scarcity. It is the natural companion citation to Clink's gibbon work when you argue that primate PAM has been running on ImageNet CNNs while the bird world moved to bioacoustic embeddings.
- https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajp.23666

**Hard-negative / acoustically-similar-species training as a precision remedy, and BirdNET misclassification analyses**

- Recurring practitioner and published finding that adding non-class training examples from acoustically similar species shifts classifier outputs and improves performance by increasing precision. Analyses of BirdNET misclassification attribute errors to class imbalance, signal-to-noise ratio and cross-correlation within the dataset, with species sharing low-frequency vocalisations (e.g. 'Ōma'o and 'Ua'u) showing systematic cross-species confusion. Related work examines the adverse effect of label noise on BirdNET, and uncertainty calibration of multi-label bird sound classifiers. Validation studies find BirdNET recovers 90% of human-detected species at a low confidence threshold but only 65% at a high threshold.
- Use here: Empirical support for the design choice you already made — `Colobus_confuser` (654 deployment-derived clips routed to Background at detection time) is textbook hard-negative training, and it is worth defending explicitly as a contribution rather than treating it as a workaround. The generalisation for problem 1 is direct: build a `Cernic_confuser` class the same way, seeded from the C. pogonias recording where you currently score 1.0000 on 23/31 windows, and from any other congener audio you can obtain. This is the cheapest possible fix, requires no architecture change, and can be tested inside your existing LOSO protocol immediately. Two caveats the literature attaches: it only covers congeners you have anticipated and obtained audio for, so it complements rather than replaces an open-set rejector; and the 90%-vs-65% threshold sensitivity is a reminder that all of these numbers move with the operating point — your matched-95%-recall comparison is the right way to control for that.
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574954125005035

**animal2vec and MeerKAT — self-supervised transformer for rare-event raw audio, with a large PAM reference dataset**

- Self-supervised transformer operating on raw audio, designed specifically for the sparse/rare-event structure of real PAM recordings rather than curated clips, released together with MeerKAT, a large-scale labelled meerkat (mammal) bioacoustics reference dataset. Positioned against the standard practice of training on balanced, clip-level corpora that do not resemble deployment audio. Available in the bacpipe model list; 8 kHz native sample rate, the lowest of the supported models.
- Use here: Relevant to your project mainly for its problem framing, which matches yours better than most: your deployment is 630+ hours in which target calls are rare and background dominates (26,576 Background vs 2,977 Cernic source recordings, and far more extreme after sliding-window expansion). Models pretrained on balanced clip corpora do not see that statistic; animal2vec explicitly targets it. It is also one of the few foundation-model efforts trained on and evaluated with terrestrial *mammal* PAM data rather than birds, so it is worth a comparison row for taxonomic proximity to your target even though there is no published gibbon or Cercopithecus number for it. Its 8 kHz native rate is a practical limitation — check that Cercopithecus nictitans call energy sits below 4 kHz before investing, since much of the informative structure in monkey calls may be above the Nyquist limit.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01253

**ProtoCLR, ConvNeXt-BirdSet and AudioProtoPNet — prototype- and contrastive-based bird models**

- ProtoCLR: CvT-13, 384-d (the smallest embedding here), 6 s @ 16 kHz, ~20M params, contrastive prototype learning on a Xeno-Canto/BIRB subset; the weakest linear-probing model on BEANS in the Schwinger review (~74 AUROC vs 92–96 for the leaders). ConvNeXt-BirdSet: ConvNeXt-Base, 768–1024-d, 5 s @ 32 kHz, 88M params, supervised on BirdSet; strong on BirdSet (linear ~84.6, attentive ~87.2) and competitive on BEANS (~94 linear). AudioProtoPNet: ConvNeXt-based prototypical part network, 1024-d, 5 s @ 32 kHz, with a built-in classifier; AudioProtoPNet-5 scores 0.896 on BirdSet, second only to Perch 2.0's 0.908. All three are in bacpipe.
- Use here: AudioProtoPNet is the interesting one for you beyond raw accuracy, because prototypical-part networks are *inherently interpretable* — they classify by matching learned prototype patches and can show which part of the spectrogram drove a detection. For a MethodsX paper aimed at ecologists who must trust the detector, and for diagnosing your congener failure specifically, being able to display what the model matched on when it emits 1.0000 for a C. pogonias window is worth real methodological weight — it converts an unexplained failure into an inspectable one. ConvNeXt-BirdSet is a reasonable modern-CNN comparison arm at a fraction of BirdMAE's size. ProtoCLR you can skip; the review's numbers place it clearly behind the field.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01277

**Adversarial Training Improves Generalization Under Distribution Shifts in Bioacoustics**

- Investigates whether adversarial training improves robustness under the distribution shifts characteristic of bioacoustics — the focal-recording to soundscape gap and cross-site variation — rather than the security setting adversarial training was designed for. Evaluated in the BirdSet framework, where the shift from directional single-source training audio to multi-source low-SNR soundscape evaluation is explicitly quantified.
- Use here: Speculative relative to the other entries but cheap to test and aimed squarely at problem 2, and it complements the SurfPerch mixing result: SurfPerch says change the *pretraining data*, this says change the *training objective*. In your setting the shift is not just archival-to-AudioMoth but also station-to-station — the reason all 16 LOSO folds are separate folds in the first place, and the reason your 0.6953 baseline varies across them. If you want a low-cost robustness experiment inside your existing protocol, adversarial or heavy-augmentation training of the classifier head is a single-knob change you can evaluate over the same 16 folds. Report it either way: a null result here is informative for a methods paper, and it is the kind of negative finding the survey brief explicitly asked for.
